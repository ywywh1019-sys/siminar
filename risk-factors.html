03 · Risk Factors

Tobacco leads — but it is not the only risk

60–70%

of preventable lung cancer cases are attributable to tobacco smoking worldwide.

(WHO, n.d.)

15–30×

higher risk of getting or dying from lung cancer in people who smoke vs. those who do not (U.S.).

(CDC, 2026a)

Environmental & occupational

  • Secondhand tobacco smoke
  • Indoor radon gas
  • Asbestos, arsenic, diesel exhaust, silica, chromium
  • Outdoor and household air pollution
  • Arsenic or radon in well water

Individual factors

  • Personal or family history of lung cancer
  • Previous radiation therapy to the chest
  • Certain chronic lung diseases; genetic susceptibility
  • HIV infection
  • Beta-carotene supplements in people who smoke

Important: lung cancer also occurs in people who have never smoked — sometimes with no clear cause.

Sources: WHO (n.d.); CDC (2026a); Mayo Clinic (n.d.-a).
